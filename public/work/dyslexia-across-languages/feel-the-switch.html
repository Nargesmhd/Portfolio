Your keyboard switches languages in one tap. Dyslexia doesn't.

Three short trials. Each one puts you inside a moment that bilingual people with dyslexia hit every day, in Farsi, Mandarin and English. About two minutes.

Trial 1 of 3 — Farsi

Read this sentence out loud.

Everyday written Farsi leaves out most vowel marks. Readers fill them in from memory. Here is what that feels like in English.

Ths s wht wrttn Frs fls lk. Th vwls r gn, nd y hv t gss thm.

This is what written Farsi feels like. The vowels are gone, and you have to guess them.

The real thing, first as people usually write it, then with vowel marks added:

سلام، حالت چطوره؟ فردا میبینمت.
سَلام، حالَت چِطورِه؟ فَردا میبینَمَت.

Meaning: "Hi, how are you? See you tomorrow."

What research saysVowel-free Persian behaves like an opaque script: readers decode letter by letter, which is exactly the process dyslexia disrupts (Pouretemad et al., 2011). No keyboard or reading mode offers an optional vowel-mark view.
Trial 2 of 3 — Mandarin

Pick the right character.

Typing Chinese on a phone means typing the sound in letters (pinyin), then choosing the character from a pop-up list. Every option below has the same sound.

You typed yǐ. You mean "already". Which one is it?

One more. You typed wèi. You mean "not yet".

The difference between 己 已 巳 is a single stroke length. Between 末 and 未, which of two lines is longer.

What research saysDyslexia in Chinese involves the visual processing of character shape, and uses different brain regions than dyslexia in alphabetic scripts (Siok, Perfetti, Jin & Tan, Nature, 2004). Pinyin input moves the whole difficulty into this one picker step, and heavy pinyin use is linked to weaker character reading (PNAS). The picker shows shapes only. It could show meanings.
Trial 3 of 3 — the switch

Send a text to a Farsi-speaking friend. In English letters, the way many people do.

Type this message, one word at a time, and press space after each word. Autocorrect is set to English. Watch what it does to the Farsi.

salam, khoobi? see you at 5, merci
ENtheIsee
0words typed
0changed by autocorrect
0times the prediction language flipped

Every Farsi word became a different English word. A dyslexic writer often can't tell which word autocorrect replaced, because the replacement looks about as plausible as what they typed.

What research saysAdults with dyslexia make more spelling errors in a second language and type more slowly, less accurately, with more visual checking (Annals of Dyslexia, 2025; Reading and Writing, 2025). Dyslexic error patterns differ by language (Language Resources and Evaluation, 2022). Keyboard, autocorrect and predictions each guess the language separately. Nobody owns the switch.

What this adds up to

  1. Dyslexia does not transfer between scripts. Accessibility settings should be per language, not per account.
  2. The switch moment is unowned. Keyboard, autocorrect and predictions need one shared "active language" signal.
  3. Each script breaks at a different step: decoding, spelling, picking. One "dyslexia mode" cannot cover all three.
  4. No peer-reviewed study has asked bilingual adults with dyslexia about their phones. That is the study to run next.
